How to use this sheet

Values are resolved from the active sage theme as the browser renders it, so everything here matches what is on screen today. Where an earlier value has been superseded, the older swatch is shown alongside so you can identify and refresh older collateral.

This sheet is set in the shipping palette and typefaces, so it also works as a live specimen.

01

Positioning

The Lamplit Hearth

Creative north star

Sage is the surface a parent opens at 8pm, after the kids are down, with one hard thing on their mind.

The system is built to feel like a warm kitchen with the overhead lights off and one lamp on. A calm, observed friend who has already been over to your house. Not a wellness brand, not a chatbot.

Product descriptor

A calm, capable friend in a parent's corner. Warm editorial guidance for early childhood.

Category: AI parenting companion. Audience: parents of children roughly ages 2 to 5. The reference persona throughout the product is a child named Mia, age 2.5.

The six design principles

01AI as partner, not chatbot. Walks in with confidence. Warm, direct, no nonsense. Has a point of view and shows it.
02Habits built in the first 7 days. Onboarding is the product. Sage observes for 2 days, gets specific on day 3, suggests a pathway on day 4, first growth card on day 7.
03Retention is a design problem. Every screen earns the next session. Sage stays silent when it has nothing real to say.
04Trust before utility. No surveillance UI, no ad surfaces, no upsell during sensitive moments. Trust is the ramp.
05Editorial restraint. Specificity over vibes. If deleting 30% of the copy improves it, keep deleting.
06One clear thought per screen. Squint at it. If you cannot find the primary thing in one second, it has too much.

Three named color rules

The One Ember RuleTerracotta appears on 10% or less of any given screen. Its rarity is the point. Spark and spark-soft are tints of the same ember, never a second hue.
The No-Pastel RuleTile and category tints stay muted and desaturated. Baby blue, blush pink and any pastel are forbidden. Math is deep slate, never baby blue.
The Tinted-Neutral RuleNo #000, no #fff as text, no true gray. Every neutral composes from ink (31, 42, 34) or canvas (251, 250, 247) at alpha.

The name in type

Sage

Newsreader 600 · sentence case · letter-spacing -0.02em · Ink #1F2A22 on Canvas #FBFAF7

This is how the name is set in product. For any lockup or mark, work from the design team's supplied artwork rather than typesetting it fresh.

Type rules to hold

  • Sentence case everywhere, including headlines and labels.
  • One display moment per screen or per layout. Never two.
  • Muted color de-emphasizes, not smaller size.
  • Italic serif is reserved for a single emphasis word.
  • Two title tiers only. Do not drift between them per asset.

Type rules to avoid breaking

  • No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, colons, semicolons, periods or parentheses.
  • No emoji anywhere, including in the voice itself.
  • No gradient text or background-clip effects.
  • No Title Case headlines.

Shoot brief

Real, not posedA parent at the counter at 7am, coffee in hand, toddler in the doorway.
Available lightWindow light, kitchen overhead, bedside lamp. No flash, no studio polish.
Mid-actionHands tying shoes, fingers turning a page, a parent listening with eyes down. Faces are secondary.
Real diversityRange across the actual photoset, not a token frame.
Grounded toneWarm domestic light. No saturation pump.
Children's facesOptional. Shots from behind or focused on hands are privacy-respectful and preferred.

Photography in cards

The image is atmospheric ground, not a subject photo. Subject sits low or off center. At least 50% of the frame stays calm negative space. Text sits only over quiet zones. Scrim is a bottom-up gradient only where text lives, never a full-card wash. If a photo is busy, darken and desaturate until the text wins.

Eyebrow on photoCaps, 0.65rem, +0.1em, white at 85%.
Title on photoWhite, 1.2 to 1.5rem, serif permitted.
Chips on photoFrosted. Blur 8 to 12px, white at 15 to 20%.
Hero treatmentSaturate 0.88, brightness 0.86, scale 1.04. Blur is off by direction.

Sourcing paths on record

  • Direct commission. 3 to 5 real families, day-in-the-life format, roughly $8K to $15K for 60 to 100 final selects. Highest brand quality.
  • Curated stock. Stocksy United or similar editorial libraries, roughly $50 to $200 per image. Filter hard for available light and mid-action.
  • Community contributions. Free and authentic with explicit consent, but needs a moderation pipeline.
  • Recommendation on file: commission for hero surfaces, license stock for content cards.

Imagery anti-patterns

  • Stock parent photography. A diverse couple laughing with a baby in a golden-hour kitchen is marketing, not product.
  • 3D rendered illustration as primary brand voltage.
  • Watercolor-soft illustration and floating blobs.
  • Emoji or cartoon faces in product surfaces.
  • AI-generated imagery of children's faces. A privacy and trust violation regardless of consent.

Voice and tone

Calm, empowered, not chaotic mom life

From the CMO brand brief on file: Sage walks into the room with confidence. Warm, direct, no nonsense. Not whispering reassurance from the corner. Has a point of view and it shows, but feels approachable.

Seven voice principles

Specific over generic"Mia at bedtime" beats "your child during transitions."
Observational, not coaching"It has been three nights since Mia's last bedtime meltdown." Not "Great work this week."
Present tense"Sage notices," not "Sage has noticed."
Short sentencesOne idea per sentence.
Sentence case"Pick up where you left off," not "Pick Up Where You Left Off."
Trust the readerNo happy talk, no instructional preamble. The user knows they are a parent.
Editorial restraintIf deleting 30% of the copy improves it, keep deleting.

Voice in context, straight from the source

Proactive card · correct
It has been three nights since Mia's last bedtime meltdown. Want me to reflect on what has been working?
Specific, observational, asks before assuming.
Empty state · correct
No saved scripts yet. When Sage shares a script that helps, save it here so you can find it next time.
No "oops," no "do not worry." Says what this is for and gives one next action.
Card metadata · the universal pattern
5 min · Bedtime · Mia, age 2.5
Duration, then category, then personalization. Time first because parents scan for "do I have time for this." Personalization last because it is the trust signal.
Cheerleading · rejected
Way to show up for Mia today. You have earned it.
Correct version: "Pathway complete. Mia's bedtime is steadier this week."
Brand-speak · rejected
Unlock evidence-based parenting strategies that transform your daily routine.
Correct version: "Five steps for the bedtime routine. About 25 minutes total."
AI self-reference · rejected
As an AI parenting assistant trained on...
Correct version: "It is late. How is Mia tonight?"
Generic preening · rejected
Curated, not endless. Mia's lucky.
Correct version: "More tomorrow."

Shadow · warm clay, never gray

Card · rgba(150,122,92,.07 / .09)

Shadows read as lamplight falloff. A tight dark 2014-app shadow is the failure mode.

Structural do's

  • Cards live directly on canvas. Card-on-card is forbidden.
  • Surfaces are flat at rest. Elevation responds to state, not decoration.
  • Borders are 1px hairline, full perimeter, or nothing.
  • Lead metadata with time, never with the child's name.
  • Empty states say what the surface is for and give one next action.

Structural don'ts

  • No streaks, XP, level-ups, progress bars or fake red dots. The constellation is the only progress visualization.
  • No colored accent stripes via thick left or right borders.
  • No decorative glassmorphism. Glass is for floating chrome only.
  • No three primary buttons, five shelves, or multi-column dashboards on mobile.
  • Sage's name appears in the chat title and tab bar. Nowhere else as decorative copy.
